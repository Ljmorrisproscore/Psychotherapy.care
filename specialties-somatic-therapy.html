Somatic Therapy

Why Somatic Therapy for Stress and Anxiety?

Stress and anxiety are not just thoughts - they live in the body. Tightness in the chest, a racing heart, restlessness, or a sense of shutdown can all be signs that the nervous system is activated. Somatic therapy focuses on working with these physical experiences, not only talking about them.

This approach is based on a simple idea: the body holds the imprint of past stress and trauma. Even when the mind understands that something is safe, the body may still respond as if there is danger. This is one reason anxiety can feel persistent and difficult to control.

How Somatic Therapy Helps

  • It brings attention to physical sensations, helping identify where stress and anxiety are held in the body.
  • It works with the nervous system directly, supporting the body in moving out of survival states like fight, flight, or freeze.
  • It builds awareness of subtle cues so early signs of overwhelm can be recognized before they escalate.
  • It introduces gentle, body-based techniques that support regulation, grounding, and a sense of safety.
  • It allows the body to complete stress responses that may have been interrupted in the past, reducing the need for those patterns to repeat.

Lasting, Whole-System Change

Rather than focusing only on thoughts or behaviors, somatic therapy addresses stress and anxiety at a physiological level. This creates change that feels more stable and lasting because it involves the whole system - not just the mind.

Over time, this work helps restore a sense of connection to the body, making it easier to feel calm, present, and in control. The goal is not to eliminate stress entirely, but to develop a system that can respond flexibly, recover more quickly, and no longer stay stuck in cycles of anxiety.

When the body learns that it is safe again, everything begins to shift.
